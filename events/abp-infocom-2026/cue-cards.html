Opener · and the close

"Want to see a badge that runs its own operating system?"

  1. Point at the funding QR: "We're building the first real boards now."
  2. "₹100 buys the buttons for one badge. ₹1,000 a whole circuit board."
  3. "Scan this, and your name goes on our website."
  4. Company? "Your name on the board itself, from ₹10,000." Hand a QR card.

Don't say: 24 FPS in MicroPython · Adafruit "endorsed" · "published in a journal" · Oreo.

Pitch A · playful

"It's a computer"

Students, the general public

  1. Event badges get thrown away in two days.
  2. This one you keep. Hand it over, press A.
  3. Its own OS, FaleraOS, which I wrote: apps, games, an App Store. Open the Store.
  4. It updates itself over the air, like your phone.
  5. I designed the board and wrote the OS.

"It's not a badge with a computer in it. It's a computer you wear round your neck."

Pitch B · technical

"The video trick"

Engineers, developers

  1. This chip has no business playing video: half a frame a second.
  2. So I invented a format, RV565: send only what changed. Play a clip.
  3. Half a frame a second → 24 frames a second.
  4. Adafruit featured it; the paper is on arXiv; other makers use it.
  5. Inside the OS: 12.2 FPS. 24 is the firmware path.

"I didn't make the chip faster. I made it send less."

Pitch C · big picture

"Open, and built here"

Industry, sponsors, faculty

  1. Most events can't afford their own electronic badge.
  2. So I built one anyone can use. Point at the 3D board.
  3. Certified open source hardware design, by OSHWA.
  4. Espressif, who make the chip, gave it its own USB ID.
  5. Every file is public: build one tonight.

"One student, in Kolkata, built a badge the world can copy, and it's already certified."
